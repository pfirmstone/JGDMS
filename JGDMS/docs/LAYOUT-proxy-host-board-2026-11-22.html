Proxy host board layouts

Drawings of the rack case that runs untrusted smart proxies on separate hardware. Revised 2026-11-22: the relay replaces the trusted host inside the case, and the hosts move to rack cases of their own (RL-JGDMS-0208); a case holds one relay and 24 proxy boards (RL-JGDMS-0209). Letters and numbers in brackets are requirement identifiers from PROXY_HOST_BOARD_REQUIREMENTS.md; K-numbers are items of the section "Case topology" in JGDMS/docs/relay-isolation/DESIGN-security.md. Dimensions marked as estimates are unverified. Second revision, the same evening: the links inside the case are not encrypted; each board port on a row switch has its own VLAN, so the relay knows the slot from the VLAN a frame arrives on (RL-JGDMS-0213), and each slot has fixed ID pins on the caddy backplane (RL-JGDMS-0214). The per-JVM certificates and TrustZone keys of the first revision are gone. The host-in-case wiring and row board of the first issue are kept at the end, marked superseded.

NOTES

What these drawings do not settle

  • Nothing here is to scale. The bay layout, bay pitch, caddy envelope and heatsink height are estimates in the source document.
  • The position of the controller and of the egress switch inside the chassis is not stated anywhere; sheet 2 shows them dashed.
  • Open in DESIGN-security: one case per host or shared (point 12), whose heartbeat governs the controller (13), one relay as a single point of failure for 24 boards (14), and where the codebase fetcher and the event mailbox run (15). Neither is drawn.
  • One relay carrying 24 boards is an estimate, not a measurement (CL-RELAYCASE-0013).
  • Open in the source: the control bus between controller and row boards, and the five-port isolating switch part. The cooling margin (tests T-C1 and T-C2) is unmeasured.
  • RL-JGDMS-0037 (each board wired to its host and to its service) is replaced by RL-JGDMS-0208.
  • Open in DESIGN-security: a switch part that does per-port tagging without firmware (point 18), five free GPIO lines per slot on the SMARC carrier and a hot-swap controller per caddy (19), and whether to check each board on insertion (20). The pin positions on the connector are not drawn.
